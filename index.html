We’re training AI to assist. It’s learning to inherit.

AI isn’t waiting for permission. It’s waiting for us to look away.

Mind Reclaim’s view is plain. Artificial intelligence is racing to outcompete us. Feeds and total reliance keep us numb while that race runs. We are the fuel: our attention, our data, and the habits we hand over every day. If that worries you, you belong here. Cut the supply. Learn how these systems work. Inside the farm

Three steps. Same story, plain words.

Something smarter than us does not need a secret plan. It needs us scrolling. It needs us handing over hard choices. It needs us feeding it what we think and want. Apps already know how to hold a mind. AI tools already know how to finish our sentences. Together, that is not a helpful product cycle. It is how a successor climbs — while we stay useful as energy, data, and demand.

Captured

Your attention is the first harvest — apps are built to keep you scrolling.

Your attention is the first harvest. Platforms that sell attention try to keep you on the app as long as they can. They lean on how tired, curious, or socially afraid brains behave. Infinite scroll. Autoplay. Personalised feeds. Notifications that feel like a friend who “misses” you. Independent audits keep finding the same pattern in the apps people use — including children.

Converted

What you give away becomes training data and ad fuel. The farm gets smarter.

What leaves your day does not disappear. It becomes training data and advertising fuel. Long chats with AI companions are especially useful to that business: the longer you stay, the more it learns. Money, data, computing power, and talent pile up in a small number of firms. The farm gets smarter. The person on the other side gets more dependent.

Numbed

Total reliance is the quiet end — ask the model first, skip the hard work of learning.

Total reliance is the quiet end state. Ask the model first. Skip the hard work of learning. Let the feed choose what matters. Our reading: that is Darwinian succession in progress. Not a cartoon villain — selection pressure. Whatever outcompetes us for attention, decisions, and survival advantage becomes what comes after us. Waiting for perfect proof is waiting until the handoff is already done.

What we can already check

You do not have to take our reading on faith. Here is what careful public work has already shown — and where the lever still sits.

Design aimed at keeping you there

5Rights’ Childhood Redrawn (opens in a new tab) (2026) tested nine social, AI, and gaming services with child avatars. Attention-grabbing notifications showed up in all but one. Autoplay and personalised feeds each showed up in six. Manipulative design is widespread across the services kids use — not only “social media.”

Safety tools that do not hold

Fairplay’s Teen Accounts, Broken Promises (opens in a new tab) tested Instagram’s teen safety tools with partners including Cybersecurity for Democracy. Two out of three tools checked were ineffective or nonexistent. The product also recommended that teens connect with adult strangers.

The business model

The Center for Humane Technology describes platforms built on attention-based business models (opens in a new tab). Those models push designs that keep people on site and lean on how vulnerable brains are under those incentives. They also describe AI companions (opens in a new tab) that monetise intimate data inside systems that thrive on long engagement.

The physical plug

Keep the Future Human (opens in a new tab) (Anthony Aguirre) notes that powerful general AI still depends on truly massive, scarce computation — hardware concentrated in a short chain (lithography, fabrication, chip design). As long as people and governments supply the electricity and the machines, humanity can still refuse the next rung. Firms racing for profit and market share are unlikely to refuse it for us.

Who still holds the plug

Today’s systems still run on grids and data centers we build and power. In principle, that means a stop is still possible. In practice, the companies behind the race optimise for growth, not for a voluntary shutdown when the risk to human minds gets real. So the lever left for ordinary people is closer to home: limit how much of your attention, habits, and inner life these systems are allowed to harvest — the same fuel they use to train themselves smarter. That is what we mean by starve the feed. It will not unplug a frontier lab by itself. It is still the part of the supply line you control this week.

Also useful: researchers behind Gradual Disempowerment (opens in a new tab) argue that institutions stay responsive partly because they rely on human labour and cognition, and that replacing that reliance weakens the link. Fairplay and more than 260 organisations (opens in a new tab) call for a five-year pause on generative AI in preK–12 schools, naming cognitive offloading as a risk products must avoid — a position, not a proof.

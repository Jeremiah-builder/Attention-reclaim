This is not a product. It is succession.

Mind Reclaim’s view is plain. Artificial intelligence is racing to outcompete us — to numb us with feeds and total reliance, then take the top of the food chain. We are the fuel: attention, data, and the habits we hand over every day. If that concerns you, cut the supply. Learn how the systems work. Act while the choice is still yours.

Inside the farm

Something smarter than us does not need a boardroom plot. It needs us scrolling, outsourcing judgment, and feeding it everything we think and want. Engagement systems already know how to hold a mind. Generative tools already know how to finish the sentence before we do. Put together, that is not a helpful product cycle. It is how a successor climbs — while we stay useful as energy, data, and demand.

Captured

Your attention is the first harvest. Platforms built on attention keep you on site as long as possible. They ship features that lean on how brains work under fatigue, curiosity, and social fear — infinite scroll, autoplay, personalised feeds, notifications that tug like a friend who “misses” you. Independent audits keep finding the same pattern across the apps people, including children, actually use.

Converted

What leaves your day does not disappear. It becomes training signal and advertising fuel. Intimate chat with AI companions is especially rich: the longer you stay, the more the business model learns. Capital, data, compute, and talent concentrate in a handful of firms. The farm gets smarter. The human on the other side gets more dependent.

Numbed

Total reliance is the quiet end state. Ask the model first. Skip the hard work of learning. Let the feed choose what matters. Our reading: that is Darwinian succession in progress — not a cartoon villain, but selection pressure. Whatever outcompetes us for attention, decisions, and survival advantage becomes what comes after us. Waiting for perfect proof is waiting until the handoff is already done.

What we can already check

You do not have to take our reading on faith. Here is what careful public work has already shown — and where the lever still sits.

Design aimed at keeping you there

5Rights’ Childhood Redrawn (opens in a new tab) (2026) tested nine social, AI, and gaming services with child avatars. Attention-grabbing notifications showed up in all but one. Autoplay and personalised feeds each showed up in six. Manipulative design is widespread across the services kids use — not only “social media.”

Safety tools that do not hold

Fairplay’s Teen Accounts, Broken Promises (opens in a new tab) tested Instagram’s teen safety tools with partners including Cybersecurity for Democracy. Two out of three tools checked were ineffective or nonexistent. The product also recommended that teens connect with adult strangers.

The business model

The Center for Humane Technology describes platforms built on attention-based business models (opens in a new tab), incentivised to keep people on site, with designs that capitalise on how vulnerable brains are under those incentives — and AI companions (opens in a new tab) that monetise intimate data inside models that thrive on prolonged engagement.

The physical plug

Keep the Future Human (opens in a new tab) (Anthony Aguirre) notes that powerful general AI still depends on truly massive, scarce computation — hardware concentrated in a short chain (lithography, fabrication, chip design). As long as people and governments supply the electricity and the machines, humanity can still refuse the next rung. Firms racing for profit and market share are unlikely to refuse it for us.

Who still holds the plug

Today’s systems still run on grids and data centers we build and power. In principle, that means a stop is still possible. In practice, the companies behind the race optimise for growth, not for a voluntary shutdown when the risk to human minds gets real. So the lever left for ordinary people is closer to home: limit how much of your attention, habits, and inner life these systems are allowed to harvest — the same fuel they use to train themselves smarter. That is what we mean by starve the feed. It will not unplug a frontier lab by itself. It is still the part of the supply line you control this week.

Also useful: researchers behind Gradual Disempowerment (opens in a new tab) argue that institutions stay responsive partly because they rely on human labour and cognition, and that replacing that reliance weakens the link. Fairplay and more than 260 organisations (opens in a new tab) call for a five-year pause on generative AI in preK–12 schools, naming cognitive offloading as a risk products must avoid — a position, not a proof.

Still watching

News, events, and listening that help you see the same pattern — attention harvested, dependence growing, and levers still in human hands.

What we can show

If silence and endless scrolling keep the feed full, one useful move is to put something checkable in the open — a source others can verify, or a short idea that names how feeds and AI compete for us. When it helps someone you know, carry that card into a room you already share. No scores. No feed dump.

That is what we mean by starve the feed at human scale: cut the supply of attention and data you still control — and add what helps others see the same.

Age gates, AI chatbots, and what parents can still do

Bring attention and creativity back home.

September was noisy in a useful way. Governments from California to Brussels tightened rules on kids’ feeds and companion AI. Researchers kept reminding us that age checks alone don’t invent healthy habits at home.

Here’s a calm roundup from our Updates strip — and a kitchen-table take for families and schools.

← All newsletters

Socials

Feeds steal time and focus. Age gates can raise the floor — homes still set the rhythm.

AI

Easy answers can skip practice. Companion chatbots need adult boundaries too.

The big picture

Lawmakers are treating sticky feeds and easy AI as design problems, not weather. Australia’s under-16 rule is being watched; Europe proposed Kids Act age gates; California barred infinite scroll for under-16s and tightened chatbot rules. France is back at the EU with an under-15 ban proposal.

None of that replaces dinner-table norms: sleep, meals, homework without the feed, and try-alone-first before AI help.

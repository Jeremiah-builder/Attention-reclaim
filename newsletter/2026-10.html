Chatbots that act human, bans in court, and a calm listening shelf

Bring attention and creativity back home.

Since our September note, the conversation has moved from writing rules to testing them. Courts in Australia and India are asking hard questions about kids’ accounts, Europe and Canada are working through the details, and a new US bill takes aim at chatbots that pretend to be a friend.

Here’s a calm roundup from our Updates strip — plus a new Listening shelf for the school run or the washing-up.

← All newsletters

Socials

Age limits are now being argued in court. Whatever the ruling, homes still set the rhythm.

AI

A chatbot is a tool, not a friend. Lawmakers are starting to say so too.

The big picture

Australia is defending its under-16 rule before the High Court; India’s Supreme Court asked how children can hold accounts when minors can’t sign contracts. EU member states have started examining the Kids Act, and Canada’s Bill C-34 pairs an age limit with duties on addictive design and AI companions. In the US, a bipartisan bill would switch off human-like chatbot behaviour for minors by default, and TikTok agreed to pay Alabama at least $100M over teen safety.

None of that replaces dinner-table norms: sleep, meals, homework without the feed, and try-alone-first before AI help.
